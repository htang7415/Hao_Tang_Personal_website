State management should speed up shipping, not slow it down. Redux's rigor is great for large teams, but its ceremony can feel heavy when a product squad needs to ship UI changes quickly. Zustand trims the paperwork so developers can focus on behavior instead of wiring.

**What this means for your team:**

- **Ship features faster:** Single-file stores and direct setters mean fewer layers to touch when adding or adjusting behavior.
- **Simpler onboarding:** New engineers learn the state shape in minutes instead of tracing actions, reducers, and middleware chains.
- **Performance without ceremony:** Tiny bundles and selective subscriptions keep renders tight without extra abstractions.

**How to choose with intent:**

- **Reach for Redux when:** You need strict unidirectional flow, time-travel debugging, or coordination across many teams and services.
- **Reach for Zustand when:** Product teams iterate quickly on UI state, want minimal boilerplate, and value a small bundle footprint.
- **Plan migrations safely:** Move slice by slice, keep Redux for cross-cutting concerns like auth, and introduce Zustand for isolated product areas first.

**Habits that keep state healthy either way:**

- **Co-locate selectors with features:** Avoid sprawling global stores by scoping logic near the UI that uses it.
- **Test state transitions:** Deterministic tests for reducers or store actions prevent regressions during refactors.
- **Document contracts:** Types and invariants for each slice make it clear what can change and what should never break.
