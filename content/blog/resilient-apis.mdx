Reliability is a product feature. When an API flakes during checkout or login, customers churn and teams burn time firefighting instead of shipping. Resilient services absorb traffic spikes, fail gracefully when dependencies misbehave, and keep users informed instead of blocked.

**What this means for your platform:**

- **Protect revenue during spikes:** Layered rate limits and backpressure prevent overload during launches or marketing surges so critical flows keep working.
- **Keep users informed instead of blocked:** Cache with stale-if-error and targeted fallbacks return something useful while you repair the root cause.
- **Stop cascades early:** Circuit breakers plus jittered retries keep failures contained and reduce the blast radius when a dependency slows down.

**Patterns to ship this quarter:**

- **Layered rate limits:** Per-tenant, per-endpoint, and global limits that are published in docs and observability dashboards.
- **Cache with a fallback path:** Tiered caches and stale data for non-critical reads so pages load even if the source system is unhealthy.
- **Circuit breakers and jittered retries:** Break hot loops quickly, then retry with exponential backoff only for errors that are safe to repeat.
- **SLOs with fast feedback:** Health checks, synthetic probes, and on-call runbooks that make it obvious when to shift into degraded mode.

**How to measure success:**

- **Error budgets tied to KPIs:** Map 99.9% availability to business events like successful sessions or checkouts, not just request counts.
- **P99 latency with fallback coverage:** Track how many responses come from cache vs. live systems and whether user journeys stay within target latency.
- **Mean time to mitigate:** Measure how quickly you move to a degraded-but-usable state when something fails.
